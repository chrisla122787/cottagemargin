CottageMargin is a recipe costing and profit-protection tool for bakers who sell: cottage bakers supplying cafés, weekly farmers-market vendors, and micro-bakeries running 10–30 products and buying ingredients in bulk. Enter what you actually pay, add the packaging, oven energy, your own labor, the waste, and the items that come home unsold — then price every bake to a real margin and hand buyers a clean, professional price sheet. If you bake for fun, this isn't for you — a notebook is honestly enough. If baking is income, your prices need real numbers behind them.

How it works

1

Build your ingredient library

Enter each ingredient once — the bag, box, or bottle you buy and what you paid. CottageMargin works out your true cost per gram, per cup, or per egg, across weights, volumes, and counts, using each ingredient's own grams-per-cup weight.

2

Cost the whole recipe

Ingredients are only part of it. Add packaging, oven energy, booth and delivery costs, your labor, a waste allowance, and your expected sell-through — then see the true cost of the batch and of every item you actually sell.

3

Price it & share the right sheet

Pick your target margin and get a selling price that still hits it after card fees. Send cafés a wholesale sheet and markets a retail sheet — each shows only its own prices, never your costs.

The fine print, in plain words

Privacy

Your recipes, your ingredient prices, and your bakery info are stored only in your own browser on your own device. Nothing you type into the calculator is uploaded to us or anyone else — there is no account and no tracking of your numbers. Export a backup file from the app regularly, because clearing your browser data erases what's saved here.

Billing & cancellation

Subscriptions are billed monthly through Stripe. You can cancel anytime from your Stripe receipt or the Stripe customer portal — no email to us required, no cancellation fee. When you cancel, Pro stays active until the end of the period you already paid for, and the free calculator keeps working either way.
